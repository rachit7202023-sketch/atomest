# Discovery Call Questions

**Purpose:** Questions to ask during an initial discovery call with a prospective client.  
**Principle:** Understand the real problem before proposing a solution. Never pitch on the first call without understanding the business.

---

## General (all divisions)

1. What does your business do, and who are your typical customers?
2. What systems/tools do you currently use (website, CRM, calendar, email, phone)?
3. What's working well right now? What's not?
4. If you could automate one repetitive task, what would it be?
5. Who makes decisions about technology and marketing spend?
6. What's your timeline for making a change?
7. Have you tried any automation or software tools before? What happened?
8. What would a successful outcome look like in 3 months? In 6 months?

---

## Division A — AI Voice Receptionist

1. How many calls do you receive per day/week? How many go to voicemail?
2. Are calls answered after hours and on weekends?
3. What happens when a call is missed—do customers call back, or do they call a competitor?
4. Do you book appointments over the phone? Into what calendar?
5. What are the 5–10 most common questions callers ask?
6. When should a call be transferred to a human vs. handled automatically?
7. What phone system/number do you currently use?

---

## Division B — Lead Automation

1. Where do your leads come from (website form, ads, referrals, phone)?
2. How quickly does someone contact a new lead right now?
3. Where are leads stored (spreadsheet, CRM, email inbox, paper)?
4. How many leads do you get per month? How many convert to paying customers?
5. What's your current follow-up process after a lead comes in?
6. Do you use a CRM? If so, which one?
7. Who is responsible for following up on leads?

---

## Division C — CRM & Workflow Automation

1. Where does client information live (website, email, practice software, spreadsheets)?
2. Is the same data entered in more than one place?
3. What happens when a new client signs up? Who does what?
4. What tasks or deadlines commonly get missed?
5. How do you track overdue items (follow-ups, filings, deliverables)?
6. Which tools do you want to connect (CRM, email, calendar, accounting)?
7. Who is responsible for data accuracy?

---

## Division D — AI Customer Support

1. How many customer support messages do you receive per week?
2. What are the 5–10 most common questions?
3. Who answers support right now? How much time does it take?
4. What's your typical response time?
5. Do you have a knowledge base, FAQ page, or help docs?
6. When should a customer be escalated to a human?
7. What tools do you use for support (email, chat, help desk)?

---

## Division E — Premium Business Websites

1. What is your website supposed to do (generate leads, book appointments, show credibility)?
2. How many enquiries does your current website generate per month?
3. Is your site mobile-friendly? Does it load quickly?
4. Do you have a contact form or booking system? Where do submissions go?
5. Do you show up in Google when people search for your services + location?
6. Do you have brand assets (logo, colors, photos) or do those need to be created?
7. Who will update the website content after launch?

---

## Red flags (be honest about fit)

- Client expects guaranteed results (e.g., "guaranteed 100 leads per month")
- Client has no budget but expects a full build
- Client wants Atomest to own their phone numbers, domains, or CRM
- Client expects 24/7 support without a maintenance agreement
- Client demands fake testimonials or case studies on their site

If any of these are present, be honest about what Atomest can and cannot do. It's better to decline than to overpromise.
